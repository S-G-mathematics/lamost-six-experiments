Experiment 1: Learning Curves and Setup Readiness
=================================================

Purpose
-------
Experiment 1 asks which non-neural classifiers perform best as the amount of
real fitting data grows, and whether the relative rankings of generative and
discriminative methods change across the observed budgets. The comparison is
hypothesis-neutral: no method is presumed to win, and an unsupported or failed
method remains a valid recorded outcome.

Data and predictors
-------------------
The frozen LAMOST DR10 v2 baseline contains 2,330,835 source representatives,
with labels A, F, G, K, M, GALAXY, and QSO. The six numerical predictors are
Pan-STARRS g, r, i, z, and y magnitudes, plus Gaia G magnitude. The existing
500,000-source test cohort stays fixed. The remaining 1,830,835 sources form
the development pool.

Identifiers, counterpart identifiers, spectral labels, signal-to-noise values,
and audit fields are excluded from model predictors. Correlated photometric
bands are retained. There is no correlation-based feature deletion and no
global outlier deletion or learned outlier filter.

Design and paired data allocation
---------------------------------
The planned design has nine development budgets, five repetitions, and all
seventeen methods: 9 x 5 x 17 = 765 planned method-budget-repetition cells.
The budgets are 100, 300, 1,000, 3,000, 10,000, 30,000, 100,000, 300,000, and
1,000,000 development sources.

Each budget is split into 80 percent fitting, 10 percent tuning, and 10 percent
conformal calibration. Thus the 100,000-source budget has 80,000 fitting,
10,000 tuning, and 10,000 calibration rows. Within each repetition, roles and
budgets are nested where feasible; each method at a given budget uses the same
source memberships. Sampling is without replacement within a repetition.
Different repetitions may overlap. Fit and tuning quotas remain approximately
natural-proportion, with one example per class reserved where feasible at small
budgets. Calibration is a population-representative random role and receives
no forced class quotas. Any departures are saved in role manifests.

Preprocessing is learned from the fitting role only. The current E1 runner fits
median imputation and standardization on fitting rows and applies those frozen
transforms to tuning, calibration, and test rows. No preprocessing, feature
selection, or score mapping is learned from calibration or test data.

Methods and tuning
------------------
The seventeen methods are four classical class-conditional Gaussian models
(Gaussian Naive Bayes, shared-diagonal Gaussian, shrinkage LDA, and regularized
QDA); five flexible class-conditional density models (GMM, Gaussian copula,
ARF/FORDE, KDE, and continuous Chow-Liu); and eight discriminative classifiers
(regularized logistic regression, linear SVM, RBF SVM, kNN, CART, random forest,
Extra Trees, and histogram gradient boosting).

Each cell receives ten adaptive tuning trials, or five for the four expensive
methods: RBF SVM, KDE, kNN, and ARF. ARF is included in the five-trial group due
to its observed implementation cost during development checks. At most 6,750
tuning attempts are planned across the 765 cells before failures and duplicate
proposals are accounted for: thirteen methods x ten trials plus four methods x
five trials, repeated over 45 budget-repetition pairs. The sequential seeded
search starts with three random trials. Duplicate proposals are cached, and
failed trials are recorded without unlimited replacements.

ARF's declared minimum-leaf search values are 5, 20, and 100, constrained by
the smallest fitting-class count; settings without sufficient class support
are unsupported. Five is the lower bound of this benchmark search domain, not
a minimum imposed by the underlying ARF library API.

Tuning selects configurations by balanced accuracy on the tuning role. Exact
ties use a frozen, method-specific simpler-first complexity order, then lower
measured computational cost, then deterministic trial order. Calibration
coverage and set size never break a tuning tie. Test results do not select
configurations or method representatives.

Outcomes and uncertainty
------------------------
The primary outcome is balanced accuracy, the mean of the seven class recalls.
The secondary predictive outcome is mean conformal prediction-set size at a
nominal 90 percent overall coverage target. Overall and class-wise achieved
coverage are validity diagnostics; they are reported alongside set size.

For probability-output models, the conformal score is LAC, 1 minus the
probability assigned to the candidate label. For SVMs, the declared score is
the negative native one-versus-rest margin; no tuning-label probability map is
applied. A single calibration threshold uses the finite-sample order statistic
at k = ceil((m + 1) x 0.90), with an infinite threshold if k exceeds the number
of calibration rows. Prediction sets include every label whose score is at
most that threshold. No threshold is adjusted on test data.

Report all run-level points, mean curves, observed variability, and paired
method differences within the same repetition and budget. Use 95 percent
paired bootstrap intervals with 1,000 replicates. Balanced-accuracy bootstrap
resampling is stratified by true class; set size and overall coverage use
population-row resampling with matched indices across methods. These intervals
are conditional on the fitted pipelines and fixed calibration thresholds;
they do not represent unobserved training, tuning, or calibration variation.
The 0.2 percentage-point practical-improvement reporting convention was
frozen before final test evaluation, separately from statistical uncertainty.

Resource reporting and visible failures
----------------------------------------
Record fitting and prediction times, peak memory, trial counts, selected
settings, invalid-support fallback counts, and failure reasons. Unsupported
small-budget fits, insufficient class support, convergence failures, invalid
densities, timeouts, and resource-limit failures remain visible; no method is
silently replaced by another family.

Frozen operational limits, following the 10,000-row development pilot,
are a 180-second soft phase timeout and a 6 GiB RSS stop. Separately,
RLIMIT_AS is set to process-startup virtual memory plus 6 GiB headroom; the
host-level cgroup limit is 8 GiB. One additional hard wall-clock watchdog
bounds each complete method cell: trials x (180 + 30) + 4 x 180 + 180 seconds,
giving 1,950 seconds for five-trial cells and 3,000 seconds for ten-trial
cells. The phase timeout, RSS stop, address-space ceiling, host limit, and
whole-cell watchdog are separate controls. An external phase watchdog kills
a stalled child after 210 seconds (180 seconds plus 30 seconds grace).
Prediction uses 50,000-row outer batches; exact KDE also uses bounded internal
batches. A development-only ARF inference probe scored 50,000 rows in 16.50
seconds, with finite scores and no test rows accessed.

Readiness status
----------------
The development-only pilot is complete: its final subset comprised 28 cells,
with 26 completing and two ARF cells unsupported at budgets 100 and 300.
At budget 10,000, one ARF trial reached 180 seconds; another completed in
43.36 seconds. These development checks are not test-set performance results.
The release test suite passed 32 tests plus 16 subtests.

The user has authorized execution. The main configuration, source hashes,
dependency versions, trial counts, score rules, resource limits, and 0.2-point
reporting convention were frozen before test evaluation. The full 765-cell E1
run has now been launched, in ascending budget order and one method cell at a
time. Results are incomplete until its planned cells finish or receive explicit
failure statuses. Experiments 2 through 6 remain on hold.

Freeze record: benchmark/results/master_run_config.json.
Execution logs, trial histories, predictions, conformal memberships and status
records are checkpointed by method, budget and repetition. No final E1 winner
or learning-curve crossover is claimed in this readiness note.
